FactEpisode 1 · Derivative of e to a power
The derivative of $e^{f(x)}$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(e^{f(x)}\right)=f'(x)\,e^{f(x)}$
Chain rule: let $u=f(x)$, so $y=e^{u}$. Then $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{dy}{du}\times\dfrac{du}{dx}=e^{u}\times f'(x)$.
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(e^{ax+b}\right)=a\,e^{ax+b}$
The power stays exactly the same. Multiply by the derivative of the power.

We Do 7Episode 1 · Derivative of e to a power
Let $y=\dfrac{e^{x^2}}{e^{4x}}$.
a) Show that $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2(x-2)\,e^{x^2-4x}$.
$y=e^{x^2-4x}$  (index law)
$f(x)=x^2-4x,\quad f'(x)=2x-4$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=(2x-4)e^{x^2-4x}$
$\phantom{\dfrac{dy}{dx}}=2(x-2)\,e^{x^2-4x}$
b) Hence find the exact coordinates of the point on the curve where the tangent is horizontal.
Horizontal tangent: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0$
$e^{x^2-4x}>0$ for all $x$, so $x-2=0$, i.e. $x=2$
$y=e^{2^2-4(2)}=e^{-4}$
Point: $\left(2,\,e^{-4}\right)$
