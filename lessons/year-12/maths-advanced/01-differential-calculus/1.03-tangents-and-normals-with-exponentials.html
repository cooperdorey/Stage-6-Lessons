I DoEpisode 3 · Tangents and normals
Find the equation of the tangent to $y=e^{2x}$ at the point where $x=1$.
Point: $x=1,\ y=e^{2}$, so $\left(1,\,e^{2}\right)$
Gradient: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2e^{2x}$, so $m=2e^{2}$
$y-e^{2}=2e^{2}(x-1)$
$y=2e^{2}x-e^{2}$

Find the equation of the normal to $y=e^{2x}$ at the point where $x=0$.
Point: $(0,\,1)$
Tangent gradient: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2e^{2x}=2$, so the normal has gradient $-\tfrac{1}{2}$
$y-1=-\tfrac{1}{2}(x-0)$
$y=-\tfrac{1}{2}x+1$, or $x+2y-2=0$

We Do 4Episode 3 · Tangents and normals
Find the equation of the normal to $y=e^{x^{2}}$ at the point where $x=1$. Give your answer in general form.
Point: $(1,\,e)$
Tangent gradient: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2xe^{x^{2}}=2e$, so the normal has gradient $-\dfrac{1}{2e}$
$y-e=-\dfrac{1}{2e}(x-1)$
$2ey-2e^{2}=-x+1$
$x+2ey-2e^{2}-1=0$
We Do 5Episode 3 · Tangents and normals
Show that the tangent to $y=e^{x}$ at the point where $x=a$ meets the $x$-axis at $x=a-1$.
Point: $(a,\,e^{a})$. Gradient: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=e^{x}$, so $m=e^{a}$
Tangent: $y-e^{a}=e^{a}(x-a)$
Meets the $x$-axis when $y=0$: $-e^{a}=e^{a}(x-a)$
$e^{a}\ne 0$, so $-1=x-a$, giving $x=a-1$
We Do 6Episode 3 · Tangents and normals
The tangent to $y=e^{x}$ at the point where $x=a$ passes through the origin. Find $a$ and the equation of the tangent.
Point: $(a,\,e^{a})$, gradient $m=e^{a}$
Tangent: $y-e^{a}=e^{a}(x-a)$
Through $(0,\,0)$: $-e^{a}=-ae^{a}$
$e^{a}>0$, so $a=1$
Tangent: $y-e=e(x-1)$, i.e. $y=ex$
We Do 7Episode 3 · Tangents and normals
The tangent to $y=e^{-x}$ at the point where $x=1$ meets the $x$-axis at $A$ and the $y$-axis at $B$. Find the exact area of triangle $OAB$, where $O$ is the origin.
Point: $\left(1,\,\dfrac{1}{e}\right)$. Gradient: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-e^{-x}$, so $m=-\dfrac{1}{e}$
$y-\dfrac{1}{e}=-\dfrac{1}{e}(x-1)$, so $y=\dfrac{1}{e}(2-x)$
$A$: $y=0$ gives $x=2$, so $A(2,\,0)$. $B$: $x=0$ gives $y=\dfrac{2}{e}$, so $B\left(0,\,\dfrac{2}{e}\right)$
Area $=\dfrac{1}{2}\times 2\times\dfrac{2}{e}=\dfrac{2}{e}$ square units
