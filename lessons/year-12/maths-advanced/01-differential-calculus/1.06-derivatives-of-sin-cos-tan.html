FactEpisode 6 · Derivatives of trig functions
The derivatives of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\sin x\right)=\cos x\qquad\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\cos x\right)=-\sin x$
$x$ must be in radians.
Check with a graphing app: graph $y=\sin x$ and its gradient function, and compare it with $y=\cos x$.
FactEpisode 6 · Derivatives of trig functions
The derivative of $\tan x$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\tan x\right)=\sec^{2}x,\qquad \sec x=\dfrac{1}{\cos x}$
Proof: $\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$. By the quotient rule,
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\tan x\right)=\dfrac{\cos x\cdot\cos x-\sin x\cdot(-\sin x)}{\cos^{2}x}$
$\phantom{\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\tan x\right)}=\dfrac{\cos^{2}x+\sin^{2}x}{\cos^{2}x}=\dfrac{1}{\cos^{2}x}=\sec^{2}x$
FactEpisode 6 · Derivatives of trig functions
The chain rule with trig functions
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin f(x)=f'(x)\cos f(x)$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\cos f(x)=-f'(x)\sin f(x)$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\tan f(x)=f'(x)\sec^{2}f(x)$
The angle stays the same. Multiply by the derivative of the angle. These are on the reference sheet.

Find the exact gradient of the tangent to $y=\tan 2x$ at the point where $x=\dfrac{\pi}{8}$.
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2\sec^{2}2x=\dfrac{2}{\cos^{2}2x}$
At $x=\dfrac{\pi}{8}$: $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, so $\cos^{2}\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}$
Gradient $=\dfrac{2}{\frac{1}{2}}=4$
