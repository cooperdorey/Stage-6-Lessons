I DoEpisode 9 · Choosing the rule
Differentiate $y=e^{2x}\sin 3x$, giving your answer in factorised form.
Last operation: multiplied, so product rule.
$u=e^{2x},\ u'=2e^{2x} \qquad v=\sin 3x,\ v'=3\cos 3x$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2e^{2x}\sin 3x+3e^{2x}\cos 3x$
$\phantom{\dfrac{dy}{dx}}=e^{2x}(2\sin 3x+3\cos 3x)$
We Do 1Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Differentiate $y=x^{3}e^{-2x}$, giving your answer in factorised form.
Product rule.
$u=x^{3},\ u'=3x^{2} \qquad v=e^{-2x},\ v'=-2e^{-2x}$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=3x^{2}e^{-2x}-2x^{3}e^{-2x}=x^{2}e^{-2x}(3-2x)$
We Do 2Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Differentiate $y=\dfrac{\tan x}{e^{x}}$.
Quotient rule.
$u=\tan x,\ u'=\sec^{2}x \qquad v=e^{x},\ v'=e^{x}$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{e^{x}\sec^{2}x-e^{x}\tan x}{e^{2x}}=\dfrac{\sec^{2}x-\tan x}{e^{x}}$
We Do 3Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Differentiate $y=\sin\left(e^{2x}\right)$.
Chain rule: the angle is $e^{2x}$, with derivative $2e^{2x}$.
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2e^{2x}\cos\left(e^{2x}\right)$
We Do 4Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Differentiate $y=\ln\left(x^{2}+\sin x\right)$.
Chain rule: $\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$ with $f(x)=x^{2}+\sin x$.
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{2x+\cos x}{x^{2}+\sin x}$
We Do 5Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Differentiate $y=\left(1+\ln x\right)^{4}$.
Chain rule on the outside.
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=4\left(1+\ln x\right)^{3}\times\dfrac{1}{x}=\dfrac{4\left(1+\ln x\right)^{3}}{x}$
We Do 6Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Differentiate $y=\dfrac{e^{x}}{\left(1+e^{x}\right)^{2}}$, simplifying fully.
Quotient rule, with the chain rule for $v$.
$u=e^{x},\ u'=e^{x} \qquad v=\left(1+e^{x}\right)^{2},\ v'=2e^{x}\left(1+e^{x}\right)$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{e^{x}\left(1+e^{x}\right)^{2}-e^{x}\cdot 2e^{x}\left(1+e^{x}\right)}{\left(1+e^{x}\right)^{4}}$
$\phantom{\dfrac{dy}{dx}}=\dfrac{e^{x}\left(1+e^{x}-2e^{x}\right)}{\left(1+e^{x}\right)^{3}}=\dfrac{e^{x}\left(1-e^{x}\right)}{\left(1+e^{x}\right)^{3}}$
We Do 7Episode 9 · Choosing the rule
Find the equation of the tangent to $y=\ln(\cos x)$ at the point where $x=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
Point: $y=\ln\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}=-\dfrac{1}{2}\ln 2$, so $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4},\,-\dfrac{1}{2}\ln 2\right)$
Gradient: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{-\sin x}{\cos x}=-\tan x$, so $m=-1$
$y+\dfrac{1}{2}\ln 2=-\left(x-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$
$y=-x+\dfrac{\pi}{4}-\dfrac{1}{2}\ln 2$
