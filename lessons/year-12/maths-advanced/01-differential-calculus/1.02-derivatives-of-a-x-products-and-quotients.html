FactEpisode 2 · a^x, products and quotients
The derivative of $a^{x}$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(a^{x}\right)=(\ln a)\,a^{x}$
Proof: $a^{x}=e^{x\ln a}$, so $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(e^{x\ln a}\right)=\ln a\cdot e^{x\ln a}=(\ln a)\,a^{x}$.
$\ln a$ is just a number. Check: when $a=e$, $\ln e=1$, so $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(e^{x}\right)=e^{x}$.

We Do 5Episode 2 · a^x, products and quotients
Differentiate $y=\dfrac{e^{x}}{e^{x}+1}$.
$u=e^{x},\ u'=e^{x} \qquad v=e^{x}+1,\ v'=e^{x}$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{e^{x}(e^{x}+1)-e^{x}\cdot e^{x}}{(e^{x}+1)^{2}}$
$\phantom{\dfrac{dy}{dx}}=\dfrac{e^{x}}{(e^{x}+1)^{2}}$
We Do 6Episode 2 · a^x, products and quotients
Find the exact coordinates of the points on $y=x^{2}e^{-x}$ where the tangent is horizontal.
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2xe^{-x}-x^{2}e^{-x}=xe^{-x}(2-x)$
Horizontal tangent: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0$. $e^{-x}>0$, so $x=0$ or $x=2$.
$x=0:\ y=0 \qquad x=2:\ y=4e^{-2}$
Points: $(0,\,0)$ and $\left(2,\,4e^{-2}\right)$
