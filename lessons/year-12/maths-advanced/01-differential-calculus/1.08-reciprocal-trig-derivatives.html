I DoEpisode 8 · Reciprocal trig derivatives
Show that $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\sec x\right)=\tan x\sec x$.
$y=\sec x=(\cos x)^{-1}$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-(\cos x)^{-2}\times(-\sin x)=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos^{2}x}$
$\phantom{\dfrac{dy}{dx}}=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}\times\dfrac{1}{\cos x}=\tan x\sec x$
We Do 1Episode 8 · Reciprocal trig derivatives
Show that $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\operatorname{cosec}x\right)=-\cot x\operatorname{cosec}x$.
$y=\operatorname{cosec}x=(\sin x)^{-1}$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-(\sin x)^{-2}\times\cos x=-\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin^{2}x}$
$\phantom{\dfrac{dy}{dx}}=-\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}\times\dfrac{1}{\sin x}=-\cot x\operatorname{cosec}x$
We Do 2Episode 8 · Reciprocal trig derivatives
Use the quotient rule to show that $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\cot x\right)=-\operatorname{cosec}^{2}x$.
$y=\cot x=\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{-\sin x\cdot\sin x-\cos x\cdot\cos x}{\sin^{2}x}=\dfrac{-\left(\sin^{2}x+\cos^{2}x\right)}{\sin^{2}x}$
$\phantom{\dfrac{dy}{dx}}=-\dfrac{1}{\sin^{2}x}=-\operatorname{cosec}^{2}x$
FactEpisode 8 · Reciprocal trig derivatives
The derivatives of the reciprocal trig functions
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\sec x\right)=\tan x\sec x$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\operatorname{cosec}x\right)=-\cot x\operatorname{cosec}x$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\cot x\right)=-\operatorname{cosec}^{2}x$
These are not on the reference sheet. The "co" functions get the minus sign.

Differentiate $y=x\sec x$, giving your answer in factorised form.
$u=x,\ u'=1 \qquad v=\sec x,\ v'=\tan x\sec x$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\sec x+x\tan x\sec x=\sec x(1+x\tan x)$
We Do 6Episode 8 · Reciprocal trig derivatives
Find the equation of the tangent to $y=\operatorname{cosec}x$ at the point where $x=\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.
Point: $\operatorname{cosec}\dfrac{\pi}{6}=2$, so $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6},\,2\right)$
Gradient: $-\cot\dfrac{\pi}{6}\operatorname{cosec}\dfrac{\pi}{6}=-\sqrt{3}\times 2=-2\sqrt{3}$
$y-2=-2\sqrt{3}\left(x-\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$
$y=-2\sqrt{3}x+\dfrac{\sqrt{3}\pi}{3}+2$
We Do 7Episode 8 · Reciprocal trig derivatives
Find the gradient of the normal to $y=2\cot x$ at the point where $x=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-2\operatorname{cosec}^{2}x$
At $x=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$: $\operatorname{cosec}\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\sqrt{2}$, so the tangent gradient is $-2\times 2=-4$
Normal gradient $=\dfrac{1}{4}$
