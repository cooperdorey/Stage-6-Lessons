FactEpisode 4 · Derivative of ln f(x)
The derivative of $\ln x$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\ln x\right)=\dfrac{1}{x},\quad x>0$
Proof: differentiate both sides of $e^{\ln x}=x$.
$e^{\ln x}\times\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\ln x\right)=1$, so $x\times\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\ln x\right)=1$, giving $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\ln x\right)=\dfrac{1}{x}$.
$\ln x$ only exists for $x>0$, so its derivative does too.

FactEpisode 4 · Derivative of ln f(x)
The derivative of $\ln f(x)$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\ln f(x)\right)=\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$
Chain rule: let $u=f(x)$, so $y=\ln u$. Then $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{1}{u}\times f'(x)=\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$.
The derivative of the inside, over the inside.

Differentiate $y=\ln\left(x^{2}-3x+1\right)$.

$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}=\dfrac{2x-3}{x^{2}-3x+1}$

We Do 7Episode 4 · Derivative of ln f(x)
Show that $\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln\left(\dfrac{1+x}{1-x}\right)=\dfrac{2}{1-x^{2}}$.
Log law first: $y=\ln(1+x)-\ln(1-x)$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{1}{1+x}-\dfrac{-1}{1-x}=\dfrac{1}{1+x}+\dfrac{1}{1-x}$
$\phantom{\dfrac{dy}{dx}}=\dfrac{(1-x)+(1+x)}{(1+x)(1-x)}=\dfrac{2}{1-x^{2}}$
