FactEpisode 6 · Logarithmic functions
The graph of $y = \log_a x$
Points $(1, 0)$ and $(a, 1)$
Asymptote $x = 0$ (the $y$-axis)
Domain: $x > 0$
Range: all real $y$
You can only take the log of a positive number
FactEpisode 6 · Logarithmic functions
Reflections in $y = x$
$y = a^{x}$ and $y = \log_a x$ are reflections of each other in $y = x$
Swap the coordinates: $(0, 1) \leftrightarrow (1, 0)$ and $(1, a) \leftrightarrow (a, 1)$

We Do 3 · solutionEpisode 6 · Logarithmic functions · Solutions
Sketch $y = \log_{1/2} x$.
Base $\tfrac12 < 1$, so the graph decreases.
$x = 1$: $y = 0$ → $(1, 0)$
$x = \tfrac12$: $y = 1$ → $\left(\tfrac12, 1\right)$
$x = 2$: $y = -1$ → $(2, -1)$ $\left(\tfrac12\right)^{-1} = 2$
Asymptote $x = 0$
Domain: $x > 0$
We Do 4 · solutionEpisode 6 · Logarithmic functions · Solutions
Sketch $y = \log_2 x + 1$.
The $+1$ moves $y = \log_2 x$ up 1. The asymptote stays at $x = 0$.
$x$-intercept: $\log_2 x = -1$, so $x = 2^{-1} = \tfrac12$ → $\left(\tfrac12, 0\right)$
Another point: $(1, 1)$
Domain: $x > 0$
Range: all real $y$
We Do 5 · solutionEpisode 6 · Logarithmic functions · Solutions
Sketch $y = \log_2(x - 1)$.
The $-1$ inside moves $y = \log_2 x$ right 1, so the asymptote is $x = 1$.
$x$-intercept: $x - 1 = 1$, so $x = 2$ → $(2, 0)$
Another point: $x = 3$ gives $y = 1$ → $(3, 1)$
Domain: $x > 1$
