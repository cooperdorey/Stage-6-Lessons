Basic results
$\log_a a = 1$
$\log_a 1 = 0$
$\log_a a^{x} = x$
$a^{\log_a x} = x$
$a^{1} = a$ and $a^{0} = 1$. A log and a power with the same base undo each other.

FactEpisode 5 · Logarithm laws
The logarithm laws
$\log_a(mn) = \log_a m + \log_a n$
from $a^{x}a^{y} = a^{x+y}$
$\log_a \dfrac{m}{n} = \log_a m - \log_a n$
from $\dfrac{a^{x}}{a^{y}} = a^{x-y}$
$\log_a m^{n} = n\log_a m$
from $\left(a^{x}\right)^{n} = a^{nx}$
Logs turn × into +, ÷ into −, and powers into multiples
FactEpisode 5 · Logarithm laws
A reciprocal
$\log_a \dfrac{1}{x} = -\log_a x$
because $\dfrac{1}{x} = x^{-1}$

Category · ruleEpisode 5 · Logarithm laws
The laws work on what is inside one log.
They never split a sum inside a log, or a fraction of two logs.

Category · model solutionEpisode 5 · Logarithm laws · Solutions
Is this a correct use of a log law?
$\log_a(x + y) = \log_a x + \log_a y$: no, a sum inside a log does not split
$\log_a 6 = \log_a 2 + \log_a 3$: yes, product law ($6 = 2 \times 3$)
$\log_a 5^{2} = 2\log_a 5$: yes, power law
$\log_a 10 - \log_a 2 = \log_a 5$: yes, quotient law
$\dfrac{\log_a 8}{\log_a 2} = \log_a 4$: no, a fraction of two logs does not simplify
Category · your turn solutionEpisode 5 · Logarithm laws · Solutions
3.
a) $(\log_a x)^{2} = 2\log_a x$: no, the power is outside the log
b) $\log_a 15 - \log_a 3 = \log_a 5$: yes, $15 \div 3 = 5$
c) $\log_a(x - y) = \log_a x - \log_a y$: no
d) $\log_a\sqrt{x} = \tfrac12 \log_a x$: yes, $\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$

$\log_a 2 = p$, $\log_a 3 = q$. Find $\log_a 18$.
Write $18$ using only $2$ and $3$: $18 = 2 \times 3^{2}$. break into known logs
$\log_a 18 = \log_a 2 + 2\log_a 3$
$= p + 2q$
